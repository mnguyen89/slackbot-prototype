Unmoderated User Testing Plan

Slackbot Landing Experience

Study Duration: ~15-20 minutes Method: Unmoderated, task-based usability test Prototype: Combined Slack Onboarding + Slackbot DM

Study Objective

Evaluate how new users interact with the Slackbot landing page after onboarding. Understand whether the page content encourages engagement, what draws attention first, and what would motivate users to continue interacting.

Participant Criteria

  • Has used a workplace messaging tool (Slack, Teams, etc.)
  • Mix of roles: IC, manager, team lead
  • Mix of Slack familiarity: new users and returning users setting up a new workspace
  • 6-8 participants

User Premise

You just created a new Slack workspace for your team. You've named it, invited a few people, and selected a plan. Now Slack is showing you a series of screens before dropping you into your workspace.

>

Interact with the prototype as you naturally would. Think aloud as you go. There are no wrong answers.

Task 1: Onboarding Modals (4 min)

The prototype begins with two onboarding modals. Have the participant interact with them naturally.

Instruction to participant:

"Go ahead and work through what's on screen. Talk me through what you're seeing and what choices you're making."

Questions to ask after the upsell modal (first screen):
  • What was that screen asking you to do?
  • What made you choose [the button they clicked]?
  • Did you read the feature list, or did you skip past it? Why?
    • Questions to ask after the segmentation modals (org size + use cases):
  • Was it clear what "How big is your org?" was asking? Did one of those options fit you well?
  • For the checklist — did those options cover how you'd actually use Slack? Was anything missing or confusing?
  • Did answering these feel worthwhile, or more like something you'd want to skip?
  • How long would you expect this type of setup to take? Did it feel quick or drawn out?

    • Task 2: Slackbot Landing — First Impressions (2 min)

    After modals dismiss, the Slackbot DM page appears.

    Instruction to participant:

    "Without clicking anything yet, look at what's in front of you. Describe what you notice first and what you think you can do here."

    Questions to ask:
  • What's the first thing that caught your eye?
  • What do you think this page is for?
  • Does anything here feel connected to the questions you just answered?

    • Task 3: Free Exploration (4 min)

    Instruction to participant:

    "Now go ahead and explore. Click on whatever interests you. Think aloud about why you're choosing to click (or not click) certain things."

    Questions to ask after they've explored:
  • Why did you click [first thing they clicked]?
  • Was there anything you considered clicking but decided not to? What held you back?
  • Did anything surprise you about what happened when you clicked?

    • Task 4: Directed Task — Get Help Setting Up (3 min)

    Instruction to participant:

    "Imagine you want help figuring out how to organize your Slack workspace for your team. Use this page to get that help."

    Questions to ask:
  • How did you decide where to go for that?
  • Did the response give you what you needed? What was useful or not useful about it?
  • Would you follow the advice given, or would you want to do something different?

    • Task 5: Directed Task — Ask Your Own Question (3 min)

    Instruction to participant:

    "Try asking Slackbot your own question — anything you'd want to know as someone setting up Slack for the first time."

    Questions to ask:
  • What made you ask that particular question?
  • Did you notice the text input area before I prompted you? What made it clear (or not clear) that you could type there?
  • How does it feel to ask an AI assistant a question during setup — natural, awkward, useful, unnecessary?

    • Wrap-up Questions (2 min)

  • On a scale of 1-5, how likely would you be to spend time on this page on your first day in a new workspace?
  • What would make you more likely to engage with it?
  • Was there anything you wished this page offered that it didn't?
  • If you could change one thing about the entire experience — from the first modal to the landing page — what would it be?
  • Any final thoughts?

    • Success Metrics

    SignalMeasure
    Upsell engagement% who read content vs. immediately dismiss
    Segmentation clarity% who answer without hesitation or confusion
    Segmentation coverage% who say options matched their real use case
    Landing page engagement% who click at least one card
    Composer usage% who type a custom question
    Time to first actionSeconds before first click on landing page
    Personalization recognition% who notice connection between answers and content
    Return intentSelf-reported likelihood to come back (1-5)

    Observer Notes Template

    For each participant, note:

    • Upsell modal: which button they clicked, how long they spent, whether they read features
    • Org size: which they selected, any hesitation
    • Use cases: which they checked, any comments about missing options
    • Slackbot landing: first element noticed, first element clicked
    • Cards they ignored and why (if stated)
    • Any confusion or hesitation moments
    • Unprompted positive/negative reactions
    • Whether they used cards, composer, or both
    • Any mention of personalization ("this seems tailored to me")
